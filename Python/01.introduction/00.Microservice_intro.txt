What is microservice?
- Its a design approach, where an application is composed of small independent services.
- In a monolotic application there will be one "Business logic" & one "Data access layer", tightly coupled into a single system.
- In microservice application each service(performs a particular business function) are loosly coupled and has its own databases.
- Each microservice is a self-contained unit that performs a specific business function.

Key Characteristics of Microservice:
- Independence: Allows development team to develop and deploy services(specific function) within an application with out affecting entire application.
- Scalability: Scale only the services with experience more load.
- Technology diversity: Instead of developing the entire application in one language, each services can be developed on different language based on characteristic of microservice been used of.
- Resilience: Failure in one service doesn't affect entire application, Each services can be fixed in isolation.

Communication between microservice:
- http RestAPI
- RabbitMQ
- gRPC

Advantages of Microservice archietecture:
- Fast development & Deployment(Development team for each services can work independently and simultatenously)
- Improved fast isolation
- Easier scaling
- Technology flexibility

Challenges of Microservice archietecture:
- Increased complexity: Each microservice may have developed in different language and has its own dependency add complexity to the system.
- Data consistency: In distributed system,Transations span multiple services and each services has its own databases make data consistency not easy.
- Monitoring & debugging: Complex due to different services and required more sophisticated tools.
- Network Latency: Could impact latency.

When to use microservices?
- Frequent update
- independent scaling
- Large development team

When to use monolotic approach?
- For a smaller projects, a monolithic approach might be simpler and more cost effective.
- So, It is essential to evaluate your project's specific needs before deciding on a microservice architecture.

Overall:
Microservices architecture involves building software as a collection of independent, loosely coupled services.
Each service is responsible for a specific function, allowing for scalability and flexibility.
